Scheduling

📅
Pairs should follow the weekly round schedule as closely as possible. Where there are genuine scheduling difficulties, flexibility is permitted — matches may be played ahead of or behind their assigned round.
📣
Teams falling behind on games played relative to the scheduled timeline will be relentlessly hounded by Matthew Hurst until the situation is resolved.
⏰
All matches must be completed before Monday 27th July 2026. Any match not played by this date will be recorded as a no-result.
⚖️
If a match cannot be played due to the fault of one team, a 3–0 walkover win may be awarded to the opposing team. This must be reviewed and approved by the match committee — Sam Grabham, Liam Davies, Dean Fitzgerald, and Chris Woolls — to prevent any risk of match fixing.

Finals Day

🏅
Once the league stages are complete, a Finals Day will be held to determine the overall winner. Format and dates will be confirmed closer to the time.

Match Format

🎾
Matches are played over 3 sets — all 3 sets must be played in full, regardless of the score after 2 sets. The pair winning the most sets wins the match.
⏱️
All 3 sets must be played to completion within the 90 minute time slot. If the match is not finished in time, teams are responsible for booking additional court time to complete it. If one participating team doesn't want to play the additional slot, they can concede.
🏆
3 points are awarded for a match win. If a match ends 2–1, the losing pair receives 1 bonus point for taking a set.

Set & Tiebreak Rules

📊
If a set reaches 6–6, a tiebreak is played — first to 7 points wins the set. The exception is the final (3rd) set, where play continues until one pair leads by 2 clear games (e.g. 8–6, 9–7), as per standard tennis rules.
⚡
In any game, if the score reaches deuce (40–40), a golden point is played — the next point wins the game. No advantage.

Point Rules

🔴
If a player or their racket touches the net during play, they lose the point immediately.
🔴
If the ball hits a player (rather than their racket), the player who was hit loses the point.
🏠
When playing indoors, if the ball hits the ceiling, the player who hit it loses the point.

Serving

🎯
When serving, the ball must be struck below waist height. An overarm or above-waist serve is a fault.

📊 Methodology: 50,000 Monte Carlo simulations. Each team is assigned a win probability from its current win rate with Laplace smoothing (so teams with few games played aren't treated as unbeatable or hopeless). Every unplayed fixture is resolved probabilistically, each win split 60/40 between 3-0 and 2-1 scorelines, with games modelled so that game difference stays meaningful. Points are tallied under standard league rules (3 for a win, 1 bonus for losing 2-1) and the final table sorted on the same tie-breaks as the standings: points, then set difference, then game difference. The badge shows each team's projected finishing position; the percentage and bar show how often that exact position came up. A high percentage means a confident projection, not a good one. Because two teams cannot occupy the same place, the projected table is resolved as an assignment problem — the model selects the arrangement of all eight teams that is jointly most likely, so every position is filled exactly once. A team's badge may therefore sit one place away from its own single most likely finish. Updated after each round of results. For entertainment only — not financial advice.
